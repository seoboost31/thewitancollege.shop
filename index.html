Where the name comes from

The Witan met to reason together

In early medieval England, the witan was a council of advisors who met with the king to debate law, land, and succession — not a parliament exactly, but a habit of talking things through with people who knew things you didn't.

We borrowed the name because it describes how we still teach: no lecture halls of three hundred, no professor talking at a room. Just a table, a text, and enough people around it to argue properly.

Admissions

Four steps to a decision

We read every application whole — essays, transcript, and recommendations together, not scored separately.

1

Submit your application

Transcript, one essay, and two recommendations — one academic, one from anyone who's seen you argue a point.

2

Interview with a current student

A 30-minute conversation, not an evaluation — mostly so you can ask your own questions about the college.

3

Admissions council review

A small panel reads your full file together and discusses it, the same way our seminars run.

4

Decision & financial aid

Decisions and a full aid offer arrive together, so you're never choosing blind.

Campus life

Small enough to know everyone's name

A residential college built around a single quad, a library that stays open past midnight, and dinners that turn into seminars.

The Long Table

A weekly open dinner where any student can bring a question and the room argues it out over dessert.

Council Societies

Student-run debate, publishing, and reading societies, each modeled loosely on the college's own namesake.

The Old Library

Open until 1am on weekdays, with a reading room that's been quietly full since the college was founded.
